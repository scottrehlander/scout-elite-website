Every fall, the same conversation happens in rinks across Massachusetts. A parent asks "so what division are we in this year?" and gets three different answers from three different people, none of whom are sure where to look it up.

This is the page that answers it. The 2026-27 divisions are posted, and **the structure changed this year**: there's a new Elite tier above White, and the number of tiers now depends on your birth year. Below: what the new structure actually looks like, where the standings and schedules live, how the league decided who plays where (that happened back in May), and how to read all of it as a parent.

If you're brand new to Massachusetts club hockey, start with the [league landscape overview](/blog/2026/04/14/massachusetts-club-hockey-leagues-e9-ehf-guide/) first, then come back. If you want to know which organizations are in the league and where they're based, the [E9 map and organization guide](/blog/2026/04/16/massachusetts-e9-hockey-teams-map-guide/) covers all 17 Massachusetts clubs.

## The E9 in 60 Seconds

The **Elite 9 Hockey League (E9)** started as a small elite league founded by a group of charter programs, running alongside the old Boston Hockey League (BHL). In the 2025-26 season, the E9 brand absorbed the BHL entirely, and every team in the combined structure became an "E9" team. It fields both boys and girls divisions.

The footprint is wider than most parents assume, and it grew again this year. Alongside the Massachusetts clubs, the 2026-27 groups include Vermont (VT Jr. Catamounts), Maine ([Casco Bay](https://www.cascobayhockey.com/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, based at Casco Bay Arena in Falmouth), and New York ([Westchester Express](https://brewstericearena.com/youth-travel-hockey){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, out of Brewster Ice Arena). If you're weighing a program, look at its division's team list before you assume the travel is all in-state.

## The 2026-27 Structure Changed. Read This Part Twice.

If you learned the E9 tiers last season, unlearn them. **The league restructured for 2026-27**, and the version most parents have in their head is now a season out of date.

Here's the shift, tier by tier:

| 2025-26 | 2026-27 |
|---|---|
| White North (top), White South, Blue, Red | **Elite** (top), then White, Blue, Red |
| Groups of 10 to 27 teams | Groups of 6 to 11 teams |
| White / Blue / Red at essentially every birth year | **Two to five divisions, depending on birth year** |

Three things follow from that, and they're the parts that trip people up:

**1. Elite is the new top tier.** Last season the best teams at a birth year played in White North. This season they play in **Elite**, and White has slid down to become the second tier. A "White" team in 2026-27 is not the same level as a "White North" team in 2025-26, even though the word is the same.

**2. The number of tiers depends on your birth year.** This is the big one, and it's why a blanket tier chart no longer works:

| Birth year | 2026-27 divisions |
|---|---|
| **2012** | Elite, White, Blue |
| **2013** | Elite, White North, White South, Blue North, Blue South |
| **2014** | Elite, White, Blue, Red |
| **2015** | Elite North, Elite South, White, Blue |
| **2016** | Elite, White, Blue, Red |
| **2017** | Elite, White, Blue |
| **2018** | Elite, White |
| **Mite** | Elite, White, Blue, Red |

The Mite groups sit alongside the birth-year groups rather than under them, and they mix 2018, 2019, and 2020 teams, so a 2018 team can appear either in its own birth-year division or in a Mite one. Check both if you don't find your team the first time.

**Looking for your specific team?** We've listed [every E9 team in its 2026-27 division](/blog/e9-divisions-by-team/), all 29 boys groups and 5 girls groups, on a separate page.

**3. North and South are still not geography, and they're no longer a White-only thing.** They remain the stronger and weaker halves of a tier, decided by results. But which tier gets split now changes by age: 2013 splits **both** White and Blue, while 2015 splits **Elite** itself. Last season the split lived at White (and sometimes Blue). This season you have to check your own birth year.

The old warning still applies, just harder: **"We're an E9 team" tells you almost nothing about the level.** An E9 Red team and an E9 Elite team are in the same league and in different worlds. Always ask which division, at which birth year, this season.

> **Why the smaller groups matter.** Last season a division could hold 27 teams. This season most hold 6 to 11. Smaller groups mean you play the same opponents more often and the standings mean more, but they also mean a single bad weekend moves you further in the table than it used to.

### The girls divisions work differently

None of the Elite / White / Blue / Red vocabulary applies on the girls side, which is a frequent source of confusion when parents compare notes across a family with a son and a daughter.

Girls play by **age group with a North and South split**, not by color tier. For 2026-27 the [girls standings](https://www.elite9hockey.com/pages/standings/girls-2026-27/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} are organized as:

| Age group | 2026-27 divisions |
|---|---|
| **U14** | North, South |
| **U12** | North, South |
| **U10** | North |

Team names carry a **T1 / T2** designation (and at some clubs Major / Minor) that marks the tier within an organization, so "Advantage U14 - T1" and "Advantage U14 - T2" are two different teams at the same club and the same age. That designation, not the division name, is what tells you the level.

## What's New for 2026-27

**The division structure was rebuilt.** Covered in detail above: a new Elite tier on top, smaller groups, and a tier count that now depends on your birth year. It's the single biggest change this season, and the one most likely to cause a misunderstanding at the rink in October.

**The Jr. Falcons joined the league.** Announced in March, the [Jr. Falcons Hockey Club enters the E9 for 2026-27](https://www.elite9hockey.com/jr-falcons-hockey-club-joins-the-elite-9-for-the-2026-27-season/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, operating out of Bentley Arena in Waltham. It's a genuinely unusual setup for youth hockey: the program practices and plays every home game on a Division I college rink. They're fielding boys teams across the 2012-2019 birth years and girls teams at U8 through U14, with a single Elite roster per age level. If the name sounds familiar, we've [written about a Jr. Falcons team before](/blog/2026/01/25/preparing-players-for-high-school-hockey-jr-falcons-2013-elite/).

**The New England Knights expanded their South Shore footprint.** The league announced a [larger Knights presence on the South Shore](https://www.elite9hockey.com/new-england-knights-expand-the-elite-9-foot-print-on-south-shore){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} as well, another sign the E9 is still actively reshaping itself after the BHL consolidation.

**The league's pedigree keeps showing up.** Three E9 alumni were selected in the 2026 NHL Draft (Casey Mutryn to Seattle at 38th overall, Justin Graf to Nashville, and Myles Brosnan to Detroit), and the league launched an All-Star Weekend in 2025-26. Whatever you think of youth hockey marketing, the development track record is real. ([League news page](https://www.elite9hockey.com/news/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"})

**The girls side is a force.** This gets less attention than the boys structure, but the E9 girls league earned 4 of the 7 Massachusetts bids to the USA Hockey Tier I and Tier II National Championships in 2025-26. If you have a daughter in the program pipeline, the E9 is very much part of that conversation now.

## How the 2026-27 Divisions Got Decided: The Parity Round

If you've heard other parents mention "parity" and nodded along, here's what it actually is.

The E9 doesn't assign divisions by reputation. Each spring, teams play a **parity round**: a set of placement games that sorts every team into the division where it belongs for the following season. Win your parity games and your team plays in a stronger division next year. Struggle, and you're placed where the competition fits.

For 2026-27, the parity round ran across three weekends in May:

| Week | Dates | Who played |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | May 1-3, 2026 | Birth years 2012, 2013, 2014, 2017 |
| Week 2 | May 8-10, 2026 | Birth years 2015, 2016, 2018, 2019 |
| Week 3 | May 29-31, 2026 | Top performers from Weeks 1-2, plus teams exempt on 2025-26 standings |

A handful of teams at each age group (roughly four to seven) earned exemptions from the early weeks based on how they finished in the 2025-26 standings, then joined Week 3 to settle the top of the table. The full breakdown lives on the league's [2026-27 parity dates page](https://www.elite9hockey.com/pages/parity-round/parity-2026-27/dates/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}.

**Why this system is good for your player:** placement is earned on the ice, every year. A club can't coast on its brand, and a team that outgrows its division gets promoted into better competition instead of padding a record. When you're evaluating a program, "where did this birth year land after parity" tells you more than any label on a jacket.

## Where to Find the E9 Schedule

Schedules live on the league's official site, [elite9hockey.com](https://www.elite9hockey.com/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, under the Schedules section. **The 2026-27 pages are live:** [boys 2026-27 schedule](https://www.elite9hockey.com/pages/schedules/boys-2026-27-schedule/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and [girls 2026-27 schedule](https://www.elite9hockey.com/pages/schedules/girls-2026-27-schedule/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}.

Both pages group games by division and let you filter down to a single team, so the fastest path is to find your division from the tables above, then filter to your club's entry.

What to expect from the schedule itself:

- **Most of your games are inside your division.** That's the point of parity placement: a season of appropriately matched competition.
- **Where a tier is split North and South, the two halves cross over**, but only about once per season per opponent. A North schedule is meaningfully harder than a South schedule even though both carry the same tier name. In 2026-27 that applies to White and Blue at 2013, and to Elite at 2015.
- League play runs from early fall through winter, with playoffs and championships in March. Girls Championship Sunday for 2025-26 was played in early March, and the boys calendar runs on a similar rhythm.

## Where to Find E9 Standings (and How to Read Them)

Standings also live on [elite9hockey.com](https://www.elite9hockey.com/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, in the Standings section, organized by season and by boys, girls, and midget divisions. The 2026-27 pages are posted ([boys](https://www.elite9hockey.com/pages/standings/boys-2026-27/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, [girls](https://www.elite9hockey.com/pages/standings/girls-2026-27/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}), and right now they're the most reliable place to confirm which division your team landed in.

Every record on them currently reads 0-0-0. That's expected: the tables are published with the divisions and rosters in place, and they fill in as games get reported. **In late summer the standings pages are a division lookup, not a standings table.**

One thing worth understanding before you check them weekly: **standings and ratings answer different questions.**

- **Standings** tell you how your team is doing *within its division*. Great for playoff races. Useless for comparing across divisions or leagues, because every division is designed to be internally even.
- **Ratings**, like [MyHockeyRankings' E9 league page](https://myhockeyrankings.com/league-info?l=567){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, estimate absolute strength from scores across all competition. That's the number to look at when you want to know how an E9 Blue team compares to an EHF Upper Gold team, or whether a division move actually changed the level of hockey your player sees. We used exactly this data to build the [New England program rankings](/blog/2026/04/15/top-new-england-boys-hockey-programs-ranked/).

A 12-2 record in a weak division and a 6-8 record in a strong one can describe the same quality of team. If you internalize one thing from this section, make it that.

## The 2026-27 Calendar at a Glance

- **January-February:** coaches quietly build next year's rosters. If you're eyeing a competitive program, [reach out before February 1](/blog/2026/04/14/massachusetts-club-hockey-leagues-e9-ehf-guide/#tryout-timing-what-you-should-be-doing-right-now).
- **March:** tryouts across Massachusetts youth hockey.
- **May:** parity round sets every team's 2026-27 division (done: May 1-31, 2026).
- **Late summer:** divisions and schedules post on the league site (done: both the [boys](https://www.elite9hockey.com/pages/standings/boys-2026-27/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and [girls](https://www.elite9hockey.com/pages/standings/girls-2026-27/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} 2026-27 pages are up).
- **Early fall through winter:** league play. Standings update as games are reported.
- **March 2027:** playoffs and championship weekend.

## What This Means for Your Player

The same advice we give in every one of these guides applies double in a league that re-sorts itself annually:

1. **Judge the birth year, not the badge.** Depending on the age group, the E9 spans two to five divisions of genuinely different hockey. What matters is where *your player's team* landed and who's coaching it.
2. **Parity results are information.** A team that moved up earned it. A team that dropped isn't necessarily failing, but it's worth asking the coach how they see the season.
3. **Don't compare this year's label to last year's.** The tier names moved underneath everyone this season. A team "in White" went from top tier to second tier without playing a single worse game. If someone tells you a program slipped, check whether the team moved or the map did.
4. **Watch a practice before you commit.** Division placement changes yearly, and so, apparently, does the structure itself. A coach who teaches well is a better bet than a division label that might not survive next May.

## Make the Season Count

However the standings turn out, your player will play thirty-plus games this season, and most of them will vanish from memory by the following weekend. The families and coaches who get the most out of a season are the ones who capture a little of it: clips of the shifts worth talking about, notes on what's improving, moments a kid can actually look at and learn from.
