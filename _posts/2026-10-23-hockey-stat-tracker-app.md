### A hockey stat tracker built for cold hands and bad Wi-Fi

Every rink I've coached in has the same two problems: the signal dies somewhere between the lobby and the bench, and your fingers stop working by the second period. The tracker is built around both.

**It works fully offline.** Nothing waits on the network. You can track a whole game in a rink with no signal and lose nothing. If you're signed in, the game backs up to your account once you're back online. If you're not, it stays on the phone until you are.

**No account needed.** Download it, tap *Track a game*, and go. A free account is only for backing games up and opening your Scout Elite content.

**A goal takes three taps.** Tap the team's score, pick the scorer, the assists and who was on the ice. Your saved lines add a whole line in one tap, and lines carry over from each team's last game, so the second game of a weekend is faster than the first.

**Both teams, not just yours.** Shots, scoring chances, faceoffs, penalties, turnovers and zone entries for each side. Plus-minus falls out of the on-ice picks for every goal, so you never track it separately.

**Mistakes are cheap.** Undo removes the last entry. The Game Events tab lets you fix any event, or its time, after the fact.

**You can leave mid-game.** The game keeps running in a bar at the bottom of the app, so you can check the practice plan or a playbook and jump straight back in.

### Custom stats: track one thing, on purpose, for a few weeks

This is the feature I'm happiest about, because it's how I actually think tracking should work.

I've written before that [most youth hockey stat tracking isn't worth the hours](/blog/tracking-youth-hockey-stats/). A season-long spreadsheet of goals and assists mostly ranks kids. The tracking that changed my teams was narrow and temporary: pick the one behavior you're working on, count only that, show the players the number, and stop counting once the behavior changes. The best example I have is shot locations for goals against. One season of it showed about 80 percent coming from a small area right in front of our net, and once the kids saw that, net-front defense fixed itself.

That's not just a coaching hunch. There's solid research behind each part of it.

**Counting a behavior and showing it changes the behavior.** The best-known hockey example is a study of a university team that wanted more body checking. The captains posted each player's hit rate in the locker room after every home weekend, then added personal goals, then praise tied to the numbers. Hitting went up as soon as the posting started.[^1] The same pattern shows up in other team sports: posting practice performance with goals and quick feedback improved specific skills in high school soccer players[^2] and in college football players, where the gains carried over into games.[^3]

**Monitoring works better when it's written down and shared.** A meta-analysis of 138 experiments found that monitoring progress toward a goal improved the odds of reaching it, and the effect was larger when the progress was physically recorded and reported or made public.[^4] A tally in an app that you show the team at the next practice is exactly that.

**Keep it about the task, not the kid.** The largest review of feedback research found feedback helps on average, but more than a third of the time it made performance *worse*, and the more feedback pulled attention toward the person and away from the task, the less it helped.[^5] "We had 14 blocked shots as a team" teaches. A ranking of who blocked the fewest doesn't.

**Retire the stat before it turns into the goal.** Goodhart's law, in its best-known form: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."[^6] Leave a stat up long enough and kids start chasing the number instead of the habit. Turning it off is part of the method.

Scout Elite Live is built around that loop. Open **Settings → Event types → Add custom type**, and you can:

- **Name it** (Blocked Shot, Puck Battle Won, Stick on Puck, D-to-D Pass) with a short label for the button.
- **Track it per team** or for the game as a whole.
- **Add fields if you want detail:** a player number, a text note, or a list of choices (Left, Right, Slot).
- **Choose what a tap does:** add it instantly, or open a quick form. Add-instantly with a player field gives you a few seconds to tag the number, or you can skip it.
- **Show it in Analytics,** so it appears with the rest of the game report.
- **Hide it when you're done.** Switch it off the tracker; the games you already tracked keep it. The built-in buttons work the same way, so you can clear off anything you don't use.

My routine: one custom stat at a time, tied to whatever we're teaching that month. Two or three weekends of counting, show the team the trend, and switch it off once it stops moving.

### After the buzzer: numbers before the ice is cleaned

When the game ends, the Analytics tab already has:

- **A game report by period:** score, shots, scoring chances, faceoffs, penalties and turnovers.
- **A scoring report** with everyone on the ice for every goal.
- **Player stats for both teams:** goals, assists, plus-minus and faceoffs.

Publish the game and it's on scout-elite.com too, with charts of events over game time that show momentum swings and pressure periods. That's where tracking stops being a stat sheet and starts saving you time:

- **Clips without scrubbing.** Every event you tracked has a time, so you can import a game's events into a clip project and get clips at the moments they happened. (Video upload and clip projects are on the Solo plan.)
- **AI game reports with the stats already in.** Pick the Live game as a source when you create a game report, describe the game in a few sentences, and the AI drafts the report with the numbers included. It learns your team over the season, so the reports get sharper as you go.

A good split for a youth team: a parent or assistant tracks the game in the app, shares it, and the coach has the stats, the clips and a draft report waiting without having touched a clipboard.

### Your whole Scout Elite account, at the rink

Sign in with the same account you use on scout-elite.com and the app opens everything you have there:

- **Video:** reviews and clip playlists. Turn the phone sideways for full screen.
- **Library:** practice plans with drill diagrams and times, playbooks, drills, scouting reports and game reports.
- **Teams:** accept invitations and keep your rosters with you.
- **Notifications:** a ping when a coach shares a review, plan or report with you.

Creating and editing still happens on the website, where there's room for it. The app is for tracking games and for using everything else where the hockey actually happens.

A few honest notes on that table:

- **If your program already pays for Hudl, keep it.** A human-tagged breakdown is a good product, and it's the right call for a program with a video budget. It's a different job from live tracking: you get the numbers after you upload film, not on the bench. I wrote up the longer comparison in [Scout Elite vs HUDL](/hudl-alternative/).
- **Team-management apps** are great at schedules, availability and messaging, and live scoring is a nice extra for families following along. They aren't built to help you develop players from what happened in the game.
- **Single-purpose stat apps** vary a lot, and some are good trackers. Where they stop is the end of the game: the numbers sit in the app, disconnected from your video, your reports and your practice plans.
- **Paper still works.** If a notebook and a pencil get you to an honest picture of the game, use them. The point is the picture, not the tool.

Where Scout Elite Live is different is what happens after you track: the same game feeds your clips, your game report and next week's practice, and all of it is in your pocket at the next game.

**Is Scout Elite Live free?**
Yes. The app is free to download and use, and you don't need an account to track a game. A free Scout Elite account backs your games up. Some features on the website, like uploading game video, need a Scout Elite plan.

**Does it work without a signal at the rink?**
Yes. Everything saves on your phone as you go. If you're signed in, the game backs up when you're online again.

**Can I track my own stats?**
Yes. Settings → Event types → Add custom type. Name it, choose per team or per game, add optional fields, and turn it off when you're done. Games you already tracked keep it.

**Which devices does it run on?**
iPhone, iPad and Android phones and tablets.

**Will I lose games from the old app?**
No. Games from the previous version come with you when you update.

**Can a parent track and share with the coach?**
Yes. Anyone can track a game; publish it with a free account and share it with the coach or the team from scout-elite.com.
